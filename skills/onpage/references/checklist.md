# Content questions for one page

Source: Google, "Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content" (checked 2026-11-12), https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content. The questions are Google's; the answers are the user's page.

Answer each with yes, no or unknown, quoting the page.

1. Does the page provide original information, reporting, research or analysis?
2. Does it go beyond the obvious, or does it only restate what other results say?
3. If it uses other sources, does it add value rather than copy or rewrite them?
4. Does the title or main heading summarise the page without exaggeration?
5. After reading, would a visitor have learned enough to reach their goal?
6. Is the author or publisher clear, with background a reader can check?
7. Does it show first-hand experience (own data, screenshots, tests) where the topic calls for it?
8. Is automation or AI use disclosed where readers would expect it?
9. Are dates honest? Google lists "changing dates to seem fresh without substantive changes" as a warning sign.
10. Was the page written for people who visit the site, or mainly to attract search visits?

Any "no" on 1 to 3 is a content gap, not a formatting problem. Say so plainly and propose the missing asset (data, example, test, screenshot) instead of more words.
